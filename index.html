Connected Australia

Exploring internet usage and the digital divide across Australia.

01 - How was Australia changed?

Exploring internet usage over time.

Key finding:

Reported internet use has remained close to 99% since 2020.

The increase between 2019 and 2020 was particularly large.

02 - Where is Australia most connected?

Comparing internet access across Australian states and territories.

Key finding:

The Australian Capital Territory recorded the highest Internet Access Index (90.6), while the Northern Territory had the lowest (79.9). This 10.7 point difference highlights geographic variation in digital access across Australia.

03 - Where are digital skills strongest?

Comparing digital ability across Australian states and territories in 2020.

Key finding:

The Australian Capital Territory recorded the highest Digital Ability Index (55.7), while the Northern Territory had the lowest (46.5), a difference of 9.2 index points.

How to read this map:

Larger and darker circles indicate higher Digital Ability Index scores. The scores represent digital ability, not the number of people.

04 - Where is the urban–rural digital divide largest?

Comparing Digital Inclusion Index scores between capital cities and rural areas across six Australian states in 2020.

Key finding:

NSW recorded the largest capital city–rural digital inclusion gap (8.6 points), while WA had the smallest (5.4 points). Rural areas scored lower than capital cities in all six states.

How to read this map:

Larger and darker circles indicate a wider capital city–rural digital inclusion gap. Grey areas provide geographic context; comparable data for ACT and NT is unavailable.

05 - Which age groups face the greatest digital exclusion?

Exploring digital inclusion levels across Australian age groups in 2025.

Key finding:

Digital exclusion increases with age. Among Australians aged 75 and over, 41.4% were highly excluded, compared with just 1.8% of those aged 18–34.

How to read this heatmap:

Darker blue cells represent a higher percentage of people within each age group belonging to that digital inclusion category.

06 - How do internet access and digital ability differ by income?

Comparing Internet Access and Digital Ability Index scores across income quintiles in 2020.

Key finding:

Internet Access scores were higher than Digital Ability scores across all five income quintiles. The difference ranged from 33.3 to 38.5 index points.

How to read this chart:

Each pair of dots represents Internet Access and Digital Ability scores for one income quintile. Longer connecting lines indicate a larger difference between the two indices.

11 - How do online activities vary across age groups?

Comparing participation in online shopping, video calling and online studying across Australian age groups in 2025.

Key finding:

Online shopping remained relatively common across all age groups. However, participation in video calling and online studying was much lower among older Australians.
